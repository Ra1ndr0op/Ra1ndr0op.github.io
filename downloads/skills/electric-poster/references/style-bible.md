# Electric Poster Style Bible

Use this file as the stable style specification. It describes a visual family, not a command to reproduce any single source image.

## Core identity

The style combines experimental editorial design, rave flyers, youth-culture posters, digital zines, and new-brutalist typography. Its depth is usually graphic and 2.5D: a flat poster gains space through scale, overlap, offset copies, blur separation, cast-shadow shapes, and forced perspective.

The result should feel dense, urgent, slightly unstable, and hand-assembled while retaining a deliberate hierarchy.

## Palette

- Primary field: saturated electric blue, close to `#1116E8`.
- Primary type: hot magenta, fluorescent pink, or red-pink, close to `#FF0A70`.
- Rear planes: deep navy, blue-black, and dark violet, close to `#050638`.
- Accent: acid green used sparingly for crosses, outlines, arrows, or registration errors, close to `#A5FF3F`.
- Optional neutral: true black for readable secondary information.

Use large flat color regions and hard contrast. Describe the colors as matte fluorescent ink, screen print, or offset print. Avoid turning them into glowing tubes, chrome, glass, or soft atmospheric neon unless the user explicitly asks for that material.

## Typography

Treat type as image-making material. Prefer heavy, condensed, compact sans-serif Chinese display characters and bold Latin grotesks. Vary scale aggressively: one or two oversized anchors, medium labels, and small information fragments.

Useful transformations include:

- horizontal stretching and vertical compression;
- shallow perspective skew and forced convergence;
- horizontal slicing, tearing, and partial displacement;
- stacked duplicate layers for dark offset extrusion;
- misregistered magenta, blue, and green edges;
- occasional blur or shadow on rear copies;
- overlap with line art and silhouettes.

Keep the requested headline legible. Distortion should create tension around the letterforms, not destroy every character.

## Spatial grammar

Build three to five depth planes:

1. **Rear plane** — low-opacity blue-black photography, crowds, architecture, bodies, or environmental shadows; slightly blurred and visually quiet.
2. **Middle plane** — the dominant headline, secondary type blocks, large shapes, and black rectangles; sharp and structurally clear.
3. **Front plane** — hot-magenta continuous line art, arrows, starbursts, crosses, circles, or symbols; crisp and allowed to cross over type.
4. **Surface plane** — selective grain, print misregistration, rough masks, scan noise, and local glitch fragments.

Depth should come from occlusion, scale, sharpness, and offset. Do not add a generic 3D floor, floating holograms, or an arbitrary perspective grid unless requested.

## Composition

Prefer tall posters such as 3:4 or 2:3. Use asymmetry, uneven margins, and a high information density. Let one large title establish the main reading path, then use smaller text as rhythm and texture.

Strong layouts often use one of these arrangements:

- oversized top headline with a crowded middle and a blurred lower scene;
- diagonal or vertical type blocks around a central silhouette;
- event-flyer structure with headline, activity labels, line-art mascot, and lower callout;
- sparse central graphic with a few huge words and a dark, curved rear environment.

Do not force every area to be equally busy. A few electric-blue breathing spaces make the dense layers readable.

## Illustration and image treatment

Line art should look like a fast fluorescent marker or a continuous neon-pink contour drawing. It may be absurd, gestural, or schematic rather than polished. Background photographs should be reduced to blue-black tonal shapes, with enough contour to imply people or place but not enough detail to compete with the title.

## Surface and print behavior

Use selective, imperfect effects:

- slight screen-print grain;
- rough photocopy edges;
- local color registration errors;
- a few sliced or displaced letter fragments;
- controlled motion blur in rear layers;
- uneven opacity in shadow copies.

Do not apply a heavy glitch filter to every object. The poster should remain intentionally designed.

## What the style is not

Avoid default cyberpunk, glossy 3D advertising, chrome typography, glassmorphism, polished corporate branding, symmetrical Swiss grids, pastel editorial design, generic vaporwave, and realistic cinematic concept art.
